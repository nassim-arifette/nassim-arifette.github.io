## The problem

In January 1910 the Seine rose to 8.62 m in Paris. Flood maps for a repeat exist, but they are technical, and few people can picture what they mean for their own building.

## What it does

The guide is the Zouave of the Pont de l'Alma, the statue Parisians have long used to read the Seine's floods. Type or say an address: the Zouave flies to it in 3D, reads the official flood plan (PPRI) and gives the expected depth class, then explains what to do, with sources.

A time slider replays historic floods from 1658 to today's gauge reading, with counters for buildings and area under water. "Show my street flooded" lifts a real street photo into relief and simulates rising water. Every answer shows which tools were called and the figures they returned; the assistant does not invent numbers.

## Context

Built for the Paris DevDay Community Hackathon (Impact → Climate action). The interface is in English and the data is French open data. The street images are simulations, not forecasts.
